# Table and figure captions

Every table and figure is generated by `analysis/17_tables.R` and
`analysis/18_figures.R` from stored analysis objects. Source files are named
beside each caption so that any number can be traced to the script that
produced it.

---

## Tables

**Table 1. Weighted characteristics of the analytic sample, overall and by
quintile of healthful plant-based diet index.**
Non-pregnant adults aged ≥20 years in the fasting subsample of NHANES
2017–March 2020 with a reliable day-1 dietary recall and a complete composite
outcome (n = 3,131). Continuous variables are survey-weighted means (standard
errors); categorical variables are weighted percentages. Values are **observed,
not imputed**, and the percentage missing is reported for each characteristic so
that the table describes the sample as measured. Estimates account for the
complex survey design using the fasting subsample weight.
*Source:* `outputs/tables/T1_characteristics.csv`

**Table 2. Association of plant-based diet indices with cardiometabolic
dysfunction and with secondary biomarkers.**
Survey-weighted linear regression, adjusted for age, sex, race/ethnicity,
education, family income-to-poverty ratio, smoking status, alcohol intake,
physical activity, dietary supplement use and total energy intake. Estimates
are pooled across 20 imputed datasets by Rubin's rules. Exposures are per
standard deviation. Secondary outcomes carry Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted
p-values. The model additionally adjusting for body mass index is labelled
over-adjusted: adiposity is a mediator under the study's causal diagram and is
absent from every minimal sufficient adjustment set.
*Source:* `outputs/tables/T2_primary_secondary.csv`

**Table 3. Pre-specified isocaloric food-group substitutions.**
Differences of coefficients from a single model containing all 17 food groups,
total energy and the covariate set, with standard errors incorporating the
covariance between the two coefficients. Negative values indicate that
replacing the first food with the second is associated with lower
cardiometabolic dysfunction. Substitutions are restricted to unit-compatible
pairs; five intended swaps were not estimable and are listed separately.
*Source:* `outputs/tables/T3_substitution.csv`,
`outputs/tables/12_not_estimable_swaps.csv`

**Table 4. Correction for within-person 24-hour recall measurement error.**
The reliability ratio is estimated on hPDI residualised on the covariate set,
which is the appropriate attenuation factor when the error-prone exposure sits
alongside covariates measured without error. Day-of-week and recall-mode
differences between the two days were adjusted before estimating variance
components. Confidence intervals come from a Rao–Wu–Yue–Beaumont replicate
bootstrap (500 replicates) recomputing both the coefficient and the reliability
ratio in each replicate.
*Source:* `outputs/tables/T4_calibration.csv`

**Table 5. Pre-specified sensitivity analyses.**
All nine analyses are reported regardless of result. Percentage change is
relative to the primary estimate; "inference unchanged" indicates that the
confidence interval excludes the null in the same direction as the primary
analysis.
*Source:* `outputs/tables/T5_sensitivity_audit.csv`

**Table 6. Exploratory axis-specific associations.**
Component scores are standardised to unit weighted variance so that estimates
on the two axes are directly comparable. The sensitivity analysis substitutes
log fasting insulin for log HOMA-IR, removing the algebraic dependency between
HOMA-IR and fasting glucose.
*Source:* `outputs/tables/T6_axis_associations.csv`

## Figures

**Figure 1. Primary estimate and pre-specified sensitivity analyses.**
Points are pooled estimates and horizontal lines 95% confidence intervals for
the difference in cardiometabolic dysfunction score per standard deviation
higher healthful plant-based diet index. The primary estimate is shown as a
blue diamond; the dotted vertical line marks its value and the dashed line the
null. Analyses are grouped by the assumption each interrogates. Every
pre-specified analysis is shown whether or not it was favourable.
*Source:* `outputs/figures/F1_sensitivity_forest.png` (and `.svg`)

**Figure 2. Pre-specified isocaloric food-group substitutions.**
Estimates are differences of coefficients from the all-components model,
holding total energy constant. Filled blue diamonds survive Benjamini–Hochberg
control at 5%. The wide intervals for fruit juice and potatoes reflect limited
power rather than evidence of no association: 76% of participants reported no
legumes on the recall day.
*Source:* `outputs/figures/F2_substitution_forest.png` (and `.svg`)

**Figure 3. Individual food-group associations from the all-components model.**
Coefficients for all 17 mutually exclusive food groups, grouped by index class.
Units differ across groups and are shown beside each label; **coefficients are
not comparable across units**. Vegetable oils and animal fat are expressed per
100 g, an unusually large serving, which is why their intervals are the widest.
*Source:* `outputs/figures/F3_food_group_coefficients.png` (and `.svg`)

**Figure 4. Biomarker principal component analysis (exploratory).**
(A) Varimax-rotated loadings on the survey-weighted correlation matrix of nine
biomarkers; higher values indicate worse cardiometabolic status. (B) Scree plot
with parallel-analysis thresholds at the nominal and effective sample sizes.
Two components were retained at both. Parallel analysis has no survey-weighted
implementation, generates its null at the nominal sample size and therefore
tends to over-retain, so it is used as a heuristic supported by the scree plot
and by a priori interpretability.
*Source:* `outputs/figures/F4_pca.png` (and `.svg`)

**Figure 5. Effect of correcting for within-person recall error.**
The naive estimate uses a single day-1 recall; the calibrated estimate applies
regression calibration using the day-2 replicate. The correction is **partial**:
recall error in these data is differential with respect to adiposity, which
violates the classical assumption of non-differential error, so the calibrated
estimate remains conservative.
*Source:* `outputs/figures/F5_calibration.png` (and `.svg`)

**Figure 6. Exposure distribution and day-to-day reliability.**
(A) Distribution of the healthful plant-based diet index on day 1. (B) Two-
dimensional binned density of day-1 against day-2 index values in the replicate
subsample (n = 2,739); the dashed line is the identity. The correlation of 0.49
indicates that a single 24-hour recall captures less than half the reliable
signal in usual plant-based diet quality.
*Source:* `outputs/figures/F6_exposure_reliability.png` (and `.svg`)
