**Which plant foods carry the signal? Isocaloric food-group substitution and
measurement-error–corrected plant-based diet quality in relation to
cardiometabolic dysfunction among US adults (NHANES 2017–March 2020)**

# Abstract

**Background.** Healthful plant-based diet quality is associated with better
cardiometabolic health, but the evidence rests largely on index-level
coefficients that identify neither which foods carry the association nor how
much it is attenuated by dietary measurement error.

**Objective.** To estimate the association between healthful plant-based diet
quality and a continuous cardiometabolic dysfunction score in US adults; to
decompose it into isocaloric food-group substitutions; and to quantify
attenuation due to within-person 24-hour recall error.

**Design.** Cross-sectional analysis of 3,131 non-pregnant adults aged ≥20
years in the fasting subsample of NHANES 2017–March 2020. Healthful (hPDI),
overall (PDI) and unhealthful (uPDI) plant-based diet indices were constructed
from 24-hour recalls using a hierarchical assignment procedure in which every
edible gram contributes to exactly one of 17 food groups. The outcome was the
equally weighted mean of sex-standardised z-scores of waist circumference,
log-triglycerides, reversed HDL-cholesterol, log fasting glucose and mean
arterial pressure. Survey-weighted linear regression was adjusted for a
covariate set derived from a directed acyclic graph, with multiple imputation
(m = 20). Substitutions were estimated as differences of coefficients from a
model containing all 17 groups plus total energy. Regression calibration used
the day-2 recall as a replicate (n = 2,739).

**Results.** Each standard deviation higher hPDI was associated with a 0.065
lower cardiometabolic dysfunction score (95% CI −0.106 to −0.023); uPDI was
associated in the opposite direction (+0.050, 0.015 to 0.086). Three of five
pre-specified substitutions were associated with lower dysfunction: refined
grains for whole grains (−0.030, −0.050 to −0.011), meat for nuts (−0.023,
−0.040 to −0.007), and sugar-sweetened beverages for tea or coffee (−0.008,
−0.014 to −0.001). Within-person variance of hPDI exceeded between-person
variance; the covariate-adjusted reliability ratio was 0.39, and correcting for
recall error moved the estimate to −0.167 (−0.290 to −0.055). In an exploratory
principal component analysis the association was concentrated on an
adiposity–lipid–inflammation axis (−0.119, −0.179 to −0.060) rather than a
glycaemic axis (−0.011, −0.060 to 0.039). Across nine pre-specified sensitivity
analyses the estimate ranged from −0.055 to −0.071 and no inference changed.

**Conclusions.** In this cross-sectional study, healthful plant-based diet
quality was associated with lower cardiometabolic dysfunction, concentrated in
adiposity, lipid and inflammatory markers. Specific exchanges — refined for
whole grains, meat for nuts, sugar-sweetened beverages for tea or coffee —
accounted for the association and are directly actionable. Correction for
within-person recall error more than doubled the estimated magnitude,
indicating that single-recall analyses materially understate these
associations. These are observational associations; no causal effect is
estimated.
