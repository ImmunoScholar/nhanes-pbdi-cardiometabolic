# Introduction

Plant-based dietary patterns are widely recommended for cardiometabolic health,
but not all plant foods behave alike. The plant-based diet indices introduced by
Satija and colleagues separated healthful plant foods — whole grains, fruits,
vegetables, nuts, legumes, vegetable oils, tea and coffee — from less healthful
ones such as refined grains, potatoes, fruit juices, sugar-sweetened beverages
and sweets, and showed that the two behave in opposite directions with respect
to type 2 diabetes risk. That framework has since been applied extensively,
including in the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES),
where higher healthful plant-based diet quality is consistently associated with
more favourable cardiometabolic profiles.

The association itself is therefore not in question, and establishing it again
is not the purpose of this study. Three gaps in how it is estimated and reported
are.

**First, index coefficients are not actionable.** An estimate expressed per
standard deviation of an 18-group composite tells a reader that diet quality
matters, but not which foods to change. Substitution modelling — asking what
happens when one food replaces another at constant energy — is standard in
nutritional epidemiology and has been applied to fats, oils, dairy alternatives
and protein sources. We did not identify a study applying it *within* the
plant-based diet index framework, which is where it would translate an index
into advice.

**Second, single-day dietary error is rarely addressed.** NHANES assesses diet
by 24-hour recall, which captures one day rather than usual intake. Under a
classical error model this attenuates diet–outcome coefficients toward the null
by an amount determined by the ratio of between- to within-person variance.
Methods for correcting this are well established, and have been applied to the
Healthy Eating Index in NHANES and to dietary patterns in cohort studies through
metabolomic calibration. We did not identify an application to the plant-based
diet index family, nor a published deattenuated coefficient for plant-based diet
quality. NHANES collects a second recall in most participants, so the replicate
needed for correction is already present in the data most analyses use.

**Third, composite outcomes obscure where an association acts.** Cardiometabolic
risk is usually summarised as metabolic syndrome or an equally weighted score,
which treats blood pressure, glycaemia, adiposity, lipids and inflammation as
interchangeable contributors. They are not: confirmatory factor analyses have
repeatedly found blood pressure to load weakly on a single metabolic factor.
Whether plant-based diet quality relates to cardiometabolic dysfunction globally
or to one biological axis is therefore not answered by a composite alone.

Addressing these gaps also requires confronting two problems that arise
specifically when the index is built from 24-hour recall rather than a
food-frequency questionnaire. Recall data are decomposed by the USDA Food
Patterns Equivalents Database into constituent components, which conflicts with
the mutually exclusive food groups the index assumes; and a single day is
heavily zero-inflated for foods not eaten daily, which the original scoring
procedure was never designed to handle and for which no published convention
exists.

In this study we therefore (i) estimate the association between healthful
plant-based diet quality and a continuous cardiometabolic dysfunction score in a
nationally representative sample of US adults, using a hierarchical construction
in which every edible gram contributes to exactly one food group; (ii) decompose
that association into isocaloric food-group substitutions; (iii) quantify and
correct the attenuation caused by within-person recall error using the second
recall as a replicate; and (iv) ask, as an exploratory analysis, whether the
association is concentrated on a particular biological axis.

The design is cross-sectional and observational. We estimate associations, not
causal effects, and report the full set of pre-specified sensitivity analyses
together with the conditions under which the conclusions would not hold.
