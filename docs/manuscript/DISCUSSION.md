# Discussion

## Summary

In a nationally representative sample of 3,131 US adults, higher healthful
plant-based diet quality was associated with a lower continuous cardiometabolic
dysfunction score. Three contributions distinguish this analysis from the
substantial existing literature on plant-based diet indices, none of which
rests on the association itself being new.

First, decomposing the index into **isocaloric food-group substitutions** shows
which exchanges carry the association: replacing refined grains with whole
grains, meat with nuts, and sugar-sweetened beverages with tea or coffee. An
index coefficient — "0.065 SD per SD of hPDI" — is not something a clinician,
dietitian or participant can act on. A substitution estimate is.

Second, correcting for **within-person 24-hour recall error** using the day-2
replicate moved the coefficient from −0.065 to −0.167, a factor of 2.58.
Within-person variance exceeded between-person variance, meaning a single
recall captures less than half the reliable signal in usual plant-based diet
quality. Studies estimating these associations from a single recall are
therefore reporting substantially attenuated coefficients.

Third, an exploratory principal component analysis localised the association to
the **adiposity–lipid–inflammation axis** rather than the glycaemic axis, with
the axis-specific estimate roughly twice the composite estimate.

## Relation to existing work

The association between healthful plant-based diet quality and cardiometabolic
risk is well established and was not the object of this study. Our estimates
are directionally consistent with that literature, including the contrast
between hPDI and uPDI: higher unhealthful plant-based diet quality was
associated with *worse* cardiometabolic status, reproducing the central claim
of the Satija framework that not all plant foods behave alike.

A closely related NHANES analysis has examined plant-based dietary patterns and
fasting insulin in the 2017–2018 cycle. Our study differs in using the full
pre-pandemic file, a continuous composite outcome, substitution decomposition,
and correction for measurement error, but that work should be cited and
distinguished rather than overlooked.

Measurement-error correction of dietary indices is itself an established
methodological literature, including applications of the National Cancer
Institute method to the Healthy Eating Index in NHANES and metabolomic
calibration of dietary patterns in cohort studies. What we did not find in
searches of PubMed, OpenAlex and general web sources was its application to the
plant-based diet index family, or a reported deattenuated exposure–outcome
coefficient for plant-based diet quality. Scopus and Embase were not searchable
during protocol development, so this claim is stated as "we identified no
study" rather than as an assertion that none exists.

## Strengths

The analysis uses a nationally representative complex-survey sample with
design-based inference throughout, and the weight choice was verified against
the data rather than assumed. The adjustment set was **derived** from an
explicit causal diagram and checked programmatically against the pre-specified
covariate list, rather than assembled by convention; the same diagram
established that adiposity is a mediator and prevalent disease a descendant of
the outcome, determining how each was handled before any model was fitted.

The exposure construction is fully traceable to public source files and follows
a single stated principle — every edible gram contributes exactly once — rather
than an ad hoc hybrid that would have double-counted foods across groups.
Substitution contrasts were preceded by a collinearity gate, and the study's
scoring rule, retention criteria, interpretation rule for the PCA, and the
entire sensitivity suite were fixed in advance and reported in full whether or
not they were favourable.

Every number is generated by script from public data; the pipeline rebuilds
from raw downloads to final figures with one command, source files are pinned
by checksum, and five protocol amendments are logged with their rationale.

## Limitations

**The design is cross-sectional and no causal effect is estimated.** Diet,
adiposity and cardiometabolic status were measured at a single examination.
Reverse causation — cardiometabolic status influencing diet — cannot be
excluded, although excluding participants with prevalent cardiovascular disease
or diabetes did not attenuate the association.

**Residual confounding cannot be fully eliminated with the observed variables.**
The structural version of our causal diagram yields *no* adjustment set
composed of measured variables: education and income are children of latent
socioeconomic position, and conditioning on children of a latent common cause
does not close that backdoor. The same argument applies to smoking, alcohol,
physical activity and supplement use as imperfect proxies for an unmeasured
behavioural disposition. The E-value of 1.31 quantifies what an unmeasured
confounder would need to achieve with both exposure and outcome to explain the
association away; that threshold is modest and well within the range these
constructs could plausibly attain. **The E-value describes what would be
required; it does not show that the association is robust to confounding.**

**Measurement error is differential, so the correction is partial.**
Participants excluded as implausible energy reporters had higher BMI, lower
reported intake and higher apparent diet quality — direct evidence that recall
error depends on adiposity, itself a determinant of the outcome. Classical
regression calibration assumes non-differential error, so the corrected
estimate of −0.167 should be read as indicating that the naive estimate is
attenuated *by at least* that much, not as an unbiased estimate of the
usual-intake association.

**Statistical power is limited.** The design effect of 2.74 substantially
reduces the effective sample size, and two of the five pre-specified
substitutions — fruit juice for whole fruit, potatoes for legumes — produced
confidence intervals wide enough to be uninformative. These are not evidence of
absent associations. Legumes in particular were not consumed on the recall day
by 75% of the analytic sample.

**Unit incompatibility restricts the substitutions that can be estimated.**
Groups measured in grams, ounce-equivalents and cup-equivalents cannot be
exchanged, so several swaps of genuine interest — sweets for whole fruit, meat
for legumes — are not estimable and were reported as such rather than
approximated with an invented conversion.

**Substitution estimates are not corrected for measurement error** and are
likely attenuated more than the index, since individual food groups are
measured with more day-to-day error than the aggregate that averages them.

**The index departs from the published construct.** Seventeen groups rather
than eighteen, a scoring rule for zero-inflation that the source paper does not
specify, and a hierarchical assignment procedure all mean absolute scores are
not comparable with published values. A single classification per food code was
also forced by the absence of a survey-cycle identifier in the pre-pandemic
file.

**The composite outcome weights blood pressure equally with components that
cohere far more strongly.** Mean arterial pressure correlated 0.02 with
reversed HDL-cholesterol and loaded weakly on both principal components. Equal
weighting was chosen for transparency, knowing this; the PCA result indicates
it dilutes the signal.

**The principal component analysis and the adiposity-pathway analysis are
exploratory.** No survey-weighted implementation of parallel analysis exists,
so component retention rests on a heuristic supported by scree inspection and
prior interpretability. The pathway analysis reports no proportion mediated,
because with temporal ordering assumed rather than observed such a figure would
carry no interpretation.

**Generalisability** is limited to non-institutionalised US adults aged ≥20
years in the fasting subsample, and the Gurka metabolic syndrome severity score
could not be applied as a sensitivity outcome across all groups because
published coefficients do not exist for non-Hispanic Asian or
Other/Multiracial participants.

## Conclusion

Among US adults, higher healthful plant-based diet quality was associated with
lower cardiometabolic dysfunction, and the association was concentrated in
adiposity, lipid and inflammatory markers rather than glycaemia. Decomposition
identified specific exchanges — refined for whole grains, meat for nuts,
sugar-sweetened beverages for tea or coffee — that carry the association and
are directly actionable. Correction for within-person recall error more than
doubled the estimated magnitude, indicating that single-recall analyses of
plant-based diet quality materially understate these associations. These are
cross-sectional associations from observational data; they support the case for
prospective and, where feasible, randomised evaluation of specific food
substitutions rather than of dietary indices as a whole.
